Every investigation begins at a physical location on Earth. A map poke records the digital coordinate used to anchor an AaP3Km place. From that anchor, the sampling geography is constructed in a local metric coordinate system and carried forward as a persistent Place Biography. Coordinates and grids describe the place; they do not create the place itself.

Two grid systems, one physical place

Why compare the AaP3Km and HLS grids?

Both systems help people return to Earth observations through space and time, but they begin from different design needs. The map can show them together as controllable layers so you can see where they overlap, where their boundaries differ, and which measurements relate to the place you are studying.

Place-sampling framework

Adopt-a-Pixel 3 km

A person selects a physical location with a map poke or another documented anchor. That location becomes the center of a 3 km × 3 km Area of Interest and PSU-00. The surrounding sampling grid is then constructed from that anchor.

Physical placeExists on Earth before any coordinate or grid is assigned.
Map poke and documented anchorRecords the digital reference used to begin the investigation.
3 km × 3 km AOIProvides the larger place context.
37 PSUsPSU-00 at the anchor plus PSU-01 through PSU-36 in the surrounding design.
3,700 SSUsInterpretation locations with explicit 5 m × 5 m footprints.

Measurement-product framework

Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2

HLS places Landsat and Sentinel-2 measurements into the same modified MGRS/UTM tiling system and a common 30 m measurement grid so observations can be stacked and compared through time.

Global MGRS/UTM frameworkOrganizes product geography around the world.
HLS product tileApproximately 110 km across and identified by an MGRS-aligned tile code.
30 m measurement cellsCommon cells used by the harmonized L30 and S30 products.
Repeated acquisitionsMeasurements from different dates can be stacked at the same grid location.
They are related, but neither grid is a subdivision of the other. HLS organizes delivered satellite measurements. AaP3Km organizes a persistent place, its sampling supports, observations, interpretations, and future revisits. On the map, the two systems meet through spatial intersection and support comparison.
Question to carry into the map: Which HLS tiles and 30 m cells intersect the AaP3Km AOI, PSUs, and SSU footprints, and what does each system help you understand about the same physical place?

In the interactive exploration, the grid pyramid can be turned on or off. At broad scales it shows HLS tiles; closer in it reveals the AaP3Km AOI and PSUs; at detailed scales it can compare local HLS cells with SSU interpretation footprints.

From map poke to Traveling Archive

1 · Anchor

Select a physical location

Record the longitude and latitude created by the map poke or another documented source.

2 · Construct

Build the place-sampling grid

Create the AOI, PSU, and SSU geography in the appropriate local metric coordinate system and derive its map display.

3 · Compare

Turn on the observation grid pyramid

View HLS tiles at broad scales and compare local 30 m measurement cells with the AaP3Km supports at closer scales.

4 · Investigate

Connect observations to the place

Inspect imagery, ground observations, map-producer interpretations, dates, source roles, and spatial support.

5 · Preserve

Create the baseline Traveling Archive

Carry forward the authoritative geography, the evidence inspected so far, retrieval outcomes, open questions, provenance, and geometry audit.

Pathway 03

Continue through reSearch

A Traveling Archive preserves a Place Biography so it can be visited, reviewed, and extended. New observations and interpretations can continue the record while earlier evidence, open questions, and lineage remain visible.

Use the Visitor Center to discover places and pathways. Use Traveling Archives to explore the evidence and history of each place, then continue through reSearch when new observations or questions emerge.
