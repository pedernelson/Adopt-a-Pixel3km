STEP00 · Begin with a real place

Explore a Place and Begin Its Observation Record

Use the map to choose or revisit a real place, compare several ways it has been observed, establish its study geography, and preserve a valid starting record for later STEPs.

Your guiding question: What can the available observations help you notice about this place, and what additional evidence would you need before making a stronger explanation?
Keep these evidence roles separate
  • Observation: a measurement or photograph from a particular place and time.
  • Reference imagery: an image used to view and interpret the place.
  • Map-producer interpretation: a classified or derived product created from observations and methods.
  • Sampling geography: AOI, PSU, and SSU supports used to organize the investigation.
  • Your interpretation: a reasoned statement that cites evidence and identifies uncertainty.
1 · ExploreSearch, navigate, or choose a location on the map.

4 · ValidateReveal and check the AOI, PSUs, SSUs, CRS, and geometry.
5 · PreserveCreate a baseline Traveling Archive and continue to the next STEP.
What to do in this exploration
Record an initial place question

Use plain language. A good starting question identifies a place, something observable, and a reason to return through time.

Why this last prompt matters: one source can provide useful evidence without answering every question. Identify the next evidence that would help you check, refine, or challenge your first impression.

Technical record carried into the Traveling Archive

The archive preserves the anchor source, authoritative geometry, CRS roles, AOI/PSU/SSU supports, source identities, retrieval attempts, geometry hash, unresolved work, and the STEP00 to STEP00.5 handoff. These records support traceability; they are not the opening activity.
